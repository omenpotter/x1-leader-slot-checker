How the X1 leader slot checker works

Every result comes from live X1 Mainnet RPC responses. Nothing is cached for longer than a few seconds, and times are always labelled as estimates.

01 · RESOLVE

Identify the validator

Your key is checked as a 32-byte base58 public key. If it is a vote account, getVoteAccounts resolves it to the node identity that the leader schedule uses, along with stake, commission and delinquency.

02 · SCHEDULE

Read the leader schedule

getLeaderSchedule is called with an identity filter for the current epoch and, when available, the next. Epoch-relative indices are converted to absolute slots with getEpochSchedule and grouped into consecutive leader windows.

03 · ESTIMATE

Estimate the timing

Slot duration is averaged from getRecentPerformanceSamples (up to 15 one-minute samples). The countdown recalibrates against the live slot every 20 seconds, and the range shows the fastest and slowest recent samples.

X1 leader schedule — questions and answers

What is a leader slot on X1?

A leader slot is a slot in which one specific validator is scheduled to be the block producer. X1 uses the Solana-compatible leader schedule: during its slots the leader receives forwarded transactions and has the opportunity to produce a block. Leaders are assigned in runs of four consecutive slots, which this checker shows as "leader windows".

How is an X1 validator's leader schedule determined?

The schedule is computed for each epoch from the stake distribution using a stake-weighted pseudo-random selection, so validators with more activated stake receive proportionally more slots. X1's documentation describes stake weight as the current selection criterion, with performance- and randomness-based leader selection on its technical roadmap.

How long is an epoch on X1 Mainnet?

X1 Mainnet epochs are 216,000 slots (reported by getEpochSchedule). At recently observed slot times of roughly 365 ms that is about 22 hours; at the nominal 400 ms target it is 24 hours. Solana Mainnet uses 432,000-slot epochs, so Solana rules of thumb such as "about two days per epoch" do not apply to X1.

How can a validator check upcoming leader slots?

Paste the validator's identity or vote account above. From a validator machine you can also run solana leader-schedule --url https://rpc.mainnet.x1.xyz and filter for the identity, which returns the same epoch-relative schedule this page reads.

Why might the estimated time differ from the actual slot?

Slot duration varies with network conditions. The checker projects forward from the most recently observed slot using the average duration from recent performance samples, and shows an earliest-to-latest range based on the fastest and slowest recent samples. The further away the slot, the wider the range. Treat times as planning estimates, not guarantees.

Does a scheduled leader slot guarantee a block?

No. A scheduled slot is an opportunity. If the validator is offline, delinquent, behind the cluster, or its block does not land, the slot is skipped. The production panel compares blocks produced against leader slots elapsed this epoch.

How does X1's SVM architecture relate to Solana-compatible scheduling?

X1 is fully compatible with the Solana Virtual Machine (SVM) and runs a Solana-compatible validator client (the RPC currently reports solana-core 4.x). It therefore exposes the same JSON-RPC methods for slots, epochs and leader schedules. X1-specific parameters — epoch length, slot timing, zero-cost votes and dynamic base fees — differ from Solana Mainnet, so this checker reads them from the X1 network rather than assuming Solana values.

Do I need to connect a wallet?

No. The checker is read-only. It never requests a wallet connection, signature or transaction.

Glossary

Slot
The basic unit of time in which a block may be produced. X1 targets 400 ms; recent observed averages have been faster.
Epoch
A fixed range of slots — 216,000 on X1 Mainnet — for which one leader schedule and stake snapshot apply.
Leader schedule
The mapping of every slot in an epoch to the validator identity expected to produce its block.
Leader window
A run of consecutive leader slots assigned to the same validator, normally four.
Identity (node) key
The validator's node public key. Leader schedules are keyed by identity.
Vote account
The account that records a validator's votes and receives delegated stake. It points to one identity.
Skip rate
Share of elapsed leader slots in which the validator did not produce a block.
Delinquent
A validator whose votes have fallen too far behind the cluster; it is unlikely to produce blocks.
